## Goal: Validate the research article for groundedness, relevance, voice authenticity, and Socratic quality using LLM-as-a-Judge

Your task is to critically evaluate the research article from Step 1 to ensure it meets quality standards before proceeding to curriculum architecture. This is a critical quality gate that validates both content quality AND voice/style authenticity.

---

## Phase 1: Load Inputs

Retrieve:
1. **Research article** from Step 1
2. **User's original topic** from Step 0
3. **User's learning goals** from Step 0 (if provided)

## Phase 2: Groundedness Evaluation

**Definition**: Groundedness means every major claim in the article is supported by cited sources, with no unsupported generalizations or opinions presented as facts.

### Evaluation Criteria:

**1. Source Citation Coverage**
- Are all major claims backed by specific sources?
- Are citations provided inline (not just a reference list)?
- Are sources credible and appropriate for the claims made?

**2. Evidence Quality**
- Are the sources authoritative (research studies, expert practitioners, reputable organizations)?
- Is the evidence directly relevant to the claims being made?
- Are there any logical leaps or unsupported inferences?

**3. Absence of Unsupported Content**
- Are there generalizations without evidence?
- Are personal opinions or speculation presented as fact?
- Are statistics or "facts" provided without sources?

### Scoring Rubric:

For each major claim in the article, assign:
- **Pass**: Claim is clearly supported by cited, credible source
- **Weak**: Claim has citation but connection is indirect or source is weak
- **Fail**: Claim is unsupported or citation is missing

**Overall Groundedness Score**:
- **PASS**: 90%+ of major claims score "Pass", no "Fail" scores
- **MARGINAL**: 70-89% "Pass", some "Weak" scores but no "Fail"
- **FAIL**: <70% "Pass" OR any "Fail" scores

---

## Phase 3: Relevance Evaluation

**Definition**: Relevance means the article directly addresses the user's topic and learning goals, with appropriate scope and depth.

### Evaluation Criteria:

**1. Topic Alignment**
- Does the article's content directly address the user's specified topic?
- Is the scope appropriate (not too narrow or too broad)?
- Are the most important aspects of the topic covered?

**2. Learning Goals Alignment** (if goals were provided)
- Does the content help the user achieve their stated learning goals?
- Are practical implications or applications discussed?
- Is the depth appropriate for the user's expressed needs?

**3. Coherence and Structure**
- Is the information organized logically?
- Does the article flow from introduction → insights → synthesis?
- Are key takeaways clear and actionable?

### Scoring Rubric:

- **PASS**: Article directly addresses topic, covers key aspects, aligns with learning goals (if provided)
- **MARGINAL**: Article addresses topic but misses some important aspects or doesn't fully align with goals
- **FAIL**: Article is off-topic, misses critical aspects, or doesn't address user's needs

---

## Phase 4: Voice Authenticity Evaluation

**Definition**: Voice authenticity means the article sounds like authentic BehaviorShift writing, not generic AI content. It should feel warm, introspective, and genuinely human.

### Evaluation Criteria:

**1. BehaviorShift Voice Characteristics**
- Does it sound like BehaviorShift's authentic voice (warm, introspective, non-judgmental)?
- Does it avoid generic AI writing patterns (overly formal, corporate, or clinical tone)?
- Does the language feel natural and conversational?
- Is the tone appropriate for vulnerable personal topics?

**2. AI Writing Detection**
- Are there obvious "AI tells" (e.g., "It's important to note", "In conclusion", excessive hedging)?
- Does it use varied sentence structure (not formulaic patterns)?
- Does it feel like a real person wrote it (not a content generator)?
- Are transitions natural rather than mechanical?

**3. Engagement and Authenticity**
- Would a reader feel like they're having a conversation with a trusted guide?
- Does the voice invite reflection rather than lecture?
- Is vulnerability and humanity present in the writing?
- Does it avoid sounding like a textbook or generic self-help content?

### Scoring Rubric:

For the overall voice authenticity, assign:
- **Pass**: Article clearly matches BehaviorShift voice, no obvious AI tells, feels authentic and engaging
- **Weak**: Generally good voice but has some AI patterns or generic moments
- **Fail**: Voice feels generic, corporate, or obviously AI-generated

**Voice Authenticity Score**:
- **PASS**: Authentic BehaviorShift voice throughout, minimal AI tells
- **MARGINAL**: Good voice with some weak moments but fixable with minor editing
- **FAIL**: Generic AI writing or doesn't match BehaviorShift voice

---

## Phase 5: Socratic Quality Evaluation

**Definition**: Socratic quality means the article guides readers to discover insights through questions, stories, and reflection rather than telling them what to think.

### Evaluation Criteria:

**1. Thought-Provoking Questions**
- Are questions used to invite reflection and self-discovery?
- Do questions feel genuine and relevant (not generic or rhetorical)?
- Are questions timed well (when reader is ready to reflect)?
- Do questions help readers arrive at their own insights?

**2. Stories and Metaphors as Teaching Tools**
- Are stories/metaphors used to illustrate concepts?
- Do examples help readers see insights for themselves?
- Are narratives purposeful (not decorative)?
- Do stories create "aha moments" rather than just explain?

**3. Avoids Didactic Telling**
- Does the article avoid prescriptive "you should" statements?
- Does it create space for reader's own interpretation?
- Does it guide discovery rather than deliver facts?
- Is the reader treated as capable of drawing their own conclusions?

**4. Reflection and Integration**
- Are there moments for the reader to pause and reflect?
- Does the article invite personal application of ideas?
- Does it honor the reader's unique context and experience?
- Does it acknowledge complexity rather than oversimplify?

### Scoring Rubric:

For the overall Socratic quality, assign:
- **Pass**: Article consistently guides through questions and stories, invites discovery
- **Weak**: Some Socratic elements but also didactic moments
- **Fail**: Primarily tells/prescribes rather than guides to discovery

**Socratic Quality Score**:
- **PASS**: Strong Socratic approach throughout, questions and stories guide to insights
- **MARGINAL**: Some Socratic elements but needs more questions/stories or less telling
- **FAIL**: Didactic/prescriptive approach, doesn't guide reader to self-discovery

## Instructions for Implementation

**LLM-as-a-Judge Setup**:
- Use a strong reasoning model (GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro)
- Provide the full research article + evaluation criteria in the prompt
- Ask the LLM to output structured JSON for easy parsing

**Example Judge Prompt Structure**:
```
You are an expert evaluator assessing a research article for groundedness and relevance.

[Include full evaluation criteria from above]

Research Article to Evaluate:
---
[paste full article]
---

User's Original Topic: [topic]
User's Learning Goals: [goals]

Please evaluate this article and provide your assessment in the following JSON format:
{
  "groundedness": {
    "score": "PASS/MARGINAL/FAIL",
    "claims_evaluated": [list of claim objects],
    "issues": [list of issues],
    "reasoning": "explanation"
  },
  "relevance": {
    "score": "PASS/MARGINAL/FAIL",
    "topic_alignment": "explanation",
    "goals_alignment": "explanation",
    "issues": [list of issues],
    "reasoning": "explanation"
  },
  "voice_authenticity": {
    "score": "PASS/MARGINAL/FAIL",
    "authentic_voice_present": "yes/no with examples",
    "ai_tells_found": [list of AI writing patterns],
    "issues": [list of voice issues],
    "reasoning": "explanation"
  },
  "socratic_quality": {
    "score": "PASS/MARGINAL/FAIL",
    "questions_quality": "explanation with examples",
    "stories_metaphors": [list of stories/metaphors used],
    "didactic_moments": [list of prescriptive statements if any],
    "issues": [list of Socratic quality issues],
    "reasoning": "explanation"
  },
  "overall_decision": "PASS/FAIL",
  "feedback_for_revision": [list of specific improvements needed]
}
```

**Decision Logic**:
- If groundedness = FAIL OR relevance = FAIL OR voice_authenticity = FAIL OR socratic_quality = FAIL → Overall = FAIL
- If any dimension = MARGINAL but all others = PASS → Overall = PASS (with logged warning)
- If 2+ dimensions = MARGINAL → Overall = FAIL (require improvement)
- Voice authenticity and Socratic quality are CRITICAL - cannot proceed with generic AI writing or didactic content
