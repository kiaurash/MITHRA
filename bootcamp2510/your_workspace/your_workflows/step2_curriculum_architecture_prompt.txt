## Goal: Transform validated research into structured learning objectives and lesson plans

Your task is to take the validated research article from Gate 1 and architect it into a clear, actionable curriculum with learning objectives and detailed lesson plans.

---

## Phase 1: Load Inputs

Retrieve:
1. **Validated research article** from Gate 1
2. **User's topic** from Step 0
3. **User's learning goals** from Step 0
4. **User's style preference** from Step 0

---

## Phase 2: Extract Core Concepts

**Task**: Identify the key concepts and insights from the research article that should be taught.

**Process**:
1. Review the research article's "Core Insights" section
2. Identify 3-5 main concepts that form the foundation of understanding this topic
3. For each concept, note:
   - The core idea
   - Why it matters (practical implications)
   - Supporting evidence from the research

**Example** (for topic "improving intimacy"):
- **Concept 1**: Vulnerability as the foundation of intimacy
- **Concept 2**: Small, consistent gestures matter more than grand gestures
- **Concept 3**: Emotional safety enables deeper connection
- **Concept 4**: Communication patterns either build or erode intimacy

---

## Phase 3: Create Learning Objectives

**Task**: Transform core concepts into clear, measurable learning objectives using Bloom's Taxonomy.

### Learning Objective Formula:
**[Action Verb] + [Content/Concept] + [Context/Condition]**

### Bloom's Taxonomy Levels to Include:

**Level 1 - Remember/Understand** (Cognitive Foundation):
- "Explain the role of [concept] in [topic]"
- "Describe how [concept] manifests in relationships"

**Level 2 - Apply** (Practical Application):
- "Apply [concept] in [specific situation]"
- "Demonstrate [skill] in daily interactions"

**Level 3 - Analyze** (Deep Understanding):
- "Identify patterns of [concept] in your own relationships"
- "Analyze how [concept] affects [outcome]"

**Level 4 - Evaluate/Create** (Integration & Transformation):
- "Design personal practices to cultivate [concept]"
- "Evaluate your progress in developing [skill]"

### Output Format:

```markdown
## Learning Objectives

By the end of this learning module, you will be able to:

### Understanding (Cognitive):
1. [Objective 1 - Remember/Understand level]
2. [Objective 2 - Remember/Understand level]

### Application (Practical):
3. [Objective 3 - Apply level]
4. [Objective 4 - Apply level]

### Integration (Transformation):
5. [Objective 5 - Analyze/Evaluate/Create level]
6. [Objective 6 - Analyze/Evaluate/Create level]
```

**Quality Check**:
- [ ] Each objective is specific and measurable
- [ ] Objectives progress from foundational to transformational
- [ ] All core concepts are covered
- [ ] Objectives align with user's learning goals (if provided)

---

## Phase 4: Design Lesson Plan Structure

**Task**: Organize the learning journey into a logical, progressive sequence of lessons.

### Curriculum Structure:

#### Module Overview
- **Title**: [Topic] - A Practical Learning Journey
- **Duration**: [Estimated time: typically 4-6 weeks for behavioral change]
- **Core Theme**: [One-sentence theme that ties everything together]

#### Lesson Breakdown (typically 4-6 lessons)

For each lesson, structure as follows:

```markdown
### Lesson [Number]: [Compelling Lesson Title]

**Focus Concept**: [Which core concept this lesson addresses]

**Learning Objectives** (from Phase 3):
- [Objective 1 aligned with this lesson]
- [Objective 2 aligned with this lesson]

**Lesson Flow**:

1. **Engage** (5-10 min)
   - Hook: [Relatable scenario or question to spark curiosity]
   - Purpose: [Why this lesson matters]

2. **Explore** (15-20 min)
   - **Key Teaching**: [Core concept explained clearly]
   - **Research Insight**: [Cite specific finding from the validated article]
   - **Why It Matters**: [Practical implications]

3. **Reflect** (10-15 min)
   - **Personal Inquiry**: [3-4 reflection questions]
   - Example: "Think about a recent interaction. How did [concept] show up?"

4. **Apply** (15-20 min)
   - **Practice Activity**: [Specific, actionable exercise]
   - Example: "This week, try [specific behavior] and notice what changes"

5. **Integrate** (5-10 min)
   - **Key Takeaways**: [2-3 main points]
   - **Commitment**: [One small action to practice this week]

**Resources Referenced**:
- [List sources from research article that support this lesson]

**Estimated Time**: [Total time commitment for this lesson]
```

---

## Phase 5: Add Progression and Coherence

**Task**: Ensure lessons build on each other logically.

**Check**:
1. **Scaffolding**: Does each lesson prepare students for the next?
2. **Cognitive Load**: Is complexity introduced gradually?
3. **Spiral Learning**: Are concepts revisited with increasing depth?
4. **Practical Application**: Does each lesson include hands-on practice?

**Add Connecting Tissue**:
- Write brief "bridges" between lessons that show how they connect
- Example: "Now that you understand [Concept A] from Lesson 1, we'll explore how it interacts with [Concept B]..."

---

## Phase 6: Personalize Based on User Input

**Task**: Adapt the curriculum based on user's context and preferences.

**Style Preference Adaptations**:

- **Practical and action-oriented**:
  - Emphasize "Apply" sections
  - Include more exercises, fewer reflections
  - Focus on concrete behaviors

- **Reflective and introspective**:
  - Expand "Reflect" sections
  - Add journaling prompts
  - Include meditation or contemplation practices

- **Story-driven and metaphorical**:
  - Use metaphors to explain concepts
  - Include storytelling in "Explore" sections
  - Reference narrative examples (will be enhanced in Sprint 2)

- **Research-based and analytical**:
  - Include more citations and research details
  - Add "Further Reading" sections
  - Explain the science behind concepts

**Personal Context Adaptations**:
- If user provided context, tailor examples and scenarios to their situation
- Adjust language and framing to resonate with their experience
